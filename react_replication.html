Finding

For the paper's 540B model, ReAct > Act (40 vs 30 SR). For a frozen 9B, it inverts: Act 43 >> ReAct 10. The verbose trace shows the prompted think step rationalizes re-searching ("results don't match → search again") instead of committing to click[item], so the agent loops search↔think for all 15 steps and never buys (87/100 episodes hit the step cap). Prompted reasoning is a tax a small model can't afford. Training the reasoning (experience tuning) recovers it: full-history ET reaches SR 36 without the loop.

Prompted ReAct climbs steadily with scale (10→22→40) but stays below Act until somewhere between 27B and 540B; Act is flat-high (43→50→30). So the ReAct>Act crossover is a large-scale phenomenon — at ≤27B, prompted thinking costs more (step budget + format) than it returns.

Design-A (persistent intra-trajectory RoPE + recall) — verdict

At N=5000 (n=100), full-coverage sliding windows and intra-trajectory persistent RoPE give no task-success gain over simple truncated full-history (SR tied ~28–29; Score actually lower, 45.6 vs 54.3). Recall hurts (SR 12). Caveats: the design-A cells were wall-limited (8 h timeout, still improving on val-loss) while the baseline patience-converged, so they may be under-trained; and persistent time is designed for temporal-memory (retrieval-by-time probes), which agentic task success does not directly measure. Conclusion for the writeup: simple truncated full-history ET is the strongest agentic configuration; the temporal/recall add-ons do not improve it.
